Sumcheck as Geometric Claim Randomization

Sumcheck turns a global claim over Boolean-cube vertices into an ordinary committed evaluation claim at a verifier-random point. The useful object is not the intermediate geometry itself; it is the final randomized claim.

1. A square: four Boolean corners become one random point

For two variables, the input claim is a sum over the four Boolean corners. The first round replaces the two horizontal Boolean slices by one random interpolated slice at Y1=r1. The second round replaces that slice by one random point.

H(0,0)+H(1,0)+H(0,1)+H(1,1)=S
1

Prover sends g1(X)=H(0,X)+H(1,X). Verifier checks g1(0)+g1(1)=S.

2

Verifier samples r1. The claim is now about the orange slice: H(0,r1)+H(1,r1)=g1(r1).

3

Verifier samples r0. The remaining claim is the single point H(r0,r1).

2. A cube: vertices to plane, line, point

In three variables, the same operation is easier to see as slicing a cube. Each round removes one Boolean coordinate. The verifier checks that the two Boolean faces add to the current claim, then samples a random coordinate and moves to the interpolated face.

cube vertices -> random plane -> random line -> random point

The random slice need not be a Boolean face. It is an algebraic interpolation of the two Boolean faces, and the soundness comes from sampling the slice after the prover has sent the claimed univariate polynomial.

3. The BaseFold bridge: claim folds and codeword folds share challenges

The visual reason BaseFold and sumcheck fit together is that they fold different objects along the same random challenges.

claim geometry: cube -> random slice -> point
codeword geometry: paired symbols -> interpolated child symbols -> terminal codeword

Sumcheck outputs an ordinary randomized evaluation claim. BaseFold gives the verifier a way to authenticate that claim against the committed encoded oracle, using local interpolation checks and Merkle openings.
